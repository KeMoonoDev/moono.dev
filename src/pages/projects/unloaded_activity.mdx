## What does it do?

In Minecraft, when you go far away from chunks, they'll stop being simulated. Later when you return to those chunks,
stuff like crops will not have grown at all, baby animals will still be babies, and the items you were cooking will
still be cooking.

This mod changes that by keeping track of the last time chunks and (block-)entities were simulated.
When you come back, Unloaded Activity will know how long they've been un-simulated for, and update them based on that.
It'll be as if they've always been simulated!

This also works when you're skipping the night. Instead of waiting for your iron to cook, or for your crops to grow,
you can skip the night to speed up the process!

<br/>

## How exactly does it work?

Every time a chunk or (block-)entity gets ticked, it will save the current time and also check how long it's been
since it was last ticked. If the difference is large enough, Unloaded Activity will start simulating it to make it
catch up.

### How to simulate a lot of ticks

Things may have been unloaded for a long time. When it's time for Unloaded Activity to start simulating, it needs to
simulate thousands, or maybe even millions of ticks! All without lagging the game. How does it do that?

Instead of simulating each individual tick, it runs a quick calculation to get the result of all those ticks.
For things with no randomness, like an ageing entity, this is quite simple to do!

Instead of repeatedly adding 1 to the entities age every tick, you can simply add the tick difference to the entities age.
*Though, in reality its not quite that simple, but it's just to get the idea across.*

For more complicated things like furnaces, the idea still applies. You'll have to keep track of the fuel, ingredients
and the smelting progress, but it's still the same idea of *"instead of repeatedly adding 1.. ..simply add the tick
difference"*.

#### Probability distributions

Problems starts arriving once you want to simulate stuff with randomness in their logic. This includes things like crops,
amethyst growth, and filling cauldrons when raining. If you want to simulate several random ticks accurately without
having to run each tick, you will need to start using probability distributions.

#### Binomial distribution

For things like crops, they can be simulated with just a binomial distribution. A binomial distribution basically asks:
*"If this has probability **P** of growing per tick and we have **N** ticks, what is the probability of it growing
exactly **K** times?"*

With this, it's pretty easy to figure out how much a crop should have grown.
All we need is the probability of it growing ***(P)***, and the amount of ticks we need to simulate ***(N)***,
and then we can sample a random number from the binomial distribution to get the growth amount ***(K)***.
If the growth amount is larger than the crops max growth, simply clamp the result.

#### Negative binomial distribution

A binomial distribution only works if the probability stays the same during all growth stages. But what happens if the
probability changes? For example, if you grow watermelons or pumpkins, once the stem has fully grown, the probability
of it growing a fruit changes.

That's where we can use a negative binomial distribution. A negative binomial distribution basically
asks: *"If this has probability **P** of growing per tick and we have **R** successful growths, what is the probability
of it failing to grow exactly **K** times?"*

There are a few different ways to simulate stuff with a negative binomial distribution. What Unloaded Activity does is
it completely replaces the binomial distribution if the probability can change. It then keeps sampling the negative
binomial distribution with the current probability ***(P)***, and with 1 successful growth **(R=1)**, and then it keeps
adding up the result ***(K)*** until the sum is larger than the amount of ticks it needs to simulate.
The amount of growths would be the amount of times it was able to sample the negative binomial distribution without the
sum going over the threshold. Doing it this way allows Unloaded Activity to change the probability per growth stage, and
while having accurate results.

### Simulating with weather and day/night cycle \{#weather-and-day-night}

Certain blocks only do things during specific conditions. For example, tree saplings only try to grow if it has enough
light *(without torches, that'd only be during day time)*, and cauldrons only gets filled with water when it's raining.

What Unloaded Activity does is that it will calculate the time it takes before their conditions change, and then simulate
that time range. Afterwards, it will check the next time their conditions change, and then simulate that time range.
It keeps doing this until it's unable to simulate any further, or until it has simulated all the ticks it needs to
simulate.

Unloaded Activity will also need to keep track of when it starts/stops raining. That way, it'll be able to accurately
simulate blocks that were unloaded during rainfall.

Another way of doing this would be to calculate the amount of un-simulated ticks that happened during rainfall,
and then just do one simulation pass for those ticks. Unloaded Activity actually did this before v0.7.0, but to be
flexible enough to support many different scenarios for mod support, I had to change it.

<br/>

### Cons with this approach

All of the blocks or (block-)entities tick functions have to be remade in Unloaded Activitys own way so that it is able
to simulate several ticks quickly. Unloaded Activity never actually touches the blocks or (block-)entities tick functions.

As a consequence, mod support has to be explicitly added by me or the mod developer. If a mod developer were to add
support to Unloaded Activity, they'd have to maintain 2 different tick methods per block or (block-)entity that can
simulate.
