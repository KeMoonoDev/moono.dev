# About me

Hello! My name is **Kevin**!\
Online I'm also known as **Moono**, **MoonoDev**, **KeMoono** or **KeMoonoDev**, depending on the platform.

I have a big interest in programming, as well as learning and playing around with different technologies.
My two most favorite programming languages are **Rust** and **Lua**!

I love Rust for its memory safety guarantees and the way it forces me to write safe code. It has made me change
the way I write in other languages to try to guarantee safety. And I love Lua for being simple and easy to use,
yet powerful with the use of metatables. I love them both so much that every time I start a new project,
I'll always pick Rust (if I'm able to), and also try to integrate Lua into the project if it makes sense.

## How I got into programming \{#beginning}

When I was a child I always liked playing games. I was also interested in how the games worked, and wanted to make my
own games.

I started out on Roblox. Most of my earlier "games" on Roblox were just a bunch of free models slapped together.
They functioned more as a virtual hangout place than an actual game.

My first actual game on Roblox was called "Sword Fighters". I made it by following a [series by AlvinBlox](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vCpl5M_9mcQ)
on how to make a Roblox game. Once I was done with the series, I decided to add some of my own additions,
such as cutscenes, randomized spawn locations, and other improvements.

At the time, I didn't quite understand how the code worked. I remember having to re-watch some of AlvinBlox's videos
to understand what the code did to add the things I wanted. Over time, I got better and I was able to improve the
implementation of my earlier additions.

After that, I started making a few Discord bots in JavaScript/NodeJS. I wanted to host the bots 24/7, so I got a
Raspberry Pi 4 to be able to host my Discord bots on there, while learning Linux at the same time.

While working on the Discord bots, I really started to get more comfortable with writing code. To test my skills
at the time, I decided to create UNO inside of my Discord bot which you were able to play with others. It would
present the stack and the amount of cards people had in the channel the command got ran, and then it would DM the
participants the cards they currently had as the game progressed.

After that, I've kept working on a bunch of other projects and I've been getting more comfortable and better at
programming ever since.

(PS. Most of those other projects have remained unfinished/unreleased due to various reasons, such as the idea not
working out, my scope for the project being too big, or the project simply not being original and just a copy of
something else. But there are some good stuff in there, and I would like to talk about them more on the [Projects page](/projects).
Right now though, I'm currently focusing on my active/finished projects.)
